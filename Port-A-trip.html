Day 1

Friday, May 29

Camp Mustang Island State Park · Night 2 ✓ No move · stay put
6:30 amDawn
South Jetty sunrise walk Free · 15 min drive
One of the best sunrise spots on the Texas coast. Brown Pelicans and Royal Terns roost on the jetty rocks at first light, Black Skimmers low over the ship channel. Flat easy walk — no birding agenda required. A genuinely beautiful start for both of you.
8 am
UT Marine Science Institute Patton Center Free · open Fri 10am
855 E. Cotter Ave — free admission, open Tue–Sat 10am–4pm. Eight large aquaria of Texas coastal habitats. The standout: a submerged jetty-reef tank with a clear cylinder you can stand inside, surrounded by live red drum, sheepshead, and flounder. The adjacent Wetlands Education Center boardwalk overlooks the ship channel — White Ibis and Roseate Spoonbill common. About an hour, genuinely impressive, and one of Port Aransas's most underrated stops.

10 am–3 pm
Remote work · Coffee Waves Port Aransas Work until 3pm
1007 TX-361 — open 7am–9pm. Free WiFi confirmed by reviewers, cozy seating, great espresso, live music Friday evenings. Voted best coffee shop in Texas. 4.6★ from 1,900+ reviews. Paninis and gelato available for lunch. Your partner has the beach, shops, and Port Aransas town to explore independently while you work.
💡 Park once near Coffee Waves and walk to both the UTMSI (10 min) and Leonabelle Turnbull (8 min). No need to move the truck all day.
3–5 pm
Leonabelle Turnbull Birding Center — afternoon session Free · open at dawn
An 8-minute walk from Coffee Waves. The afternoon light on the ship channel wetlands is softer and often better for photography than harsh morning light. Work the boardwalk slowly — Roseate Spoonbill, Tricolored Heron, Black-bellied Whistling-Duck year-round. Check eBird before you go: a resident flamingo has been documented here for 3+ consecutive years and the afternoon is when it tends to be most visible along the waterway edge.
↗ Google Maps
5–6:30 pm
Paradise Pond + golden hour birding Free · 5 min away
Walk 5 minutes from Leonabelle Turnbull to Paradise Pond (408 Cut-Off Rd) — a shaded 2-acre freshwater pond with boardwalk and observation decks. The golden hour light is particularly good here for songbirds in the overhanging black willows. End the loop back at Leonabelle for the final light on the channel. Between the two sites you have a complete Port Aransas birding afternoon without moving the truck once.
7 pm⭐ Dinner
Tortuga's Saltwater Grill — the nice dinner Book ahead
429 N Alister St — open Friday from 3pm. The elevated choice on the island. Lobster rolls, fresh Gulf redfish, seafood tower, wine list. 4.4★ from 2,000+ reviews. After a full day of birding and remote work, this is your Friday reward. Call (361) 749-2739 or use OpenTable — Friday nights fill, especially with summer kicking in.
💡 Tortuga's also does Saturday brunch 9am–2pm and Sunday brunch 9am–2pm — the Port A Benny and Shrimp & Grits are consistently raved about.

Day 2

Saturday, May 30

Camp PINS optional — or stay Mustang Island See camping section for details
6:15 amDawn
PINS Malaquite Beach — dawn walk Best birding morning
Drive 15 min to Padre Island National Seashore. Malaquite Beach is vehicle-free — 4.5 miles of undisturbed Gulf shoreline at first light. Walk the tide line for shorebirds: Ruddy Turnstone, Sanderling, Wilson's Plover. Then check the small freshwater pond with NPS bird blind just south of Park HQ — one of the park's best-kept secrets for close encounters. Call the Hatchling Hotline (361) 949-7163 before you go: late May occasionally produces early sea turtle hatchings.
💡 Check BirdCast the night before. A north wind overnight means lingering migrants on the dunes — late May still produces surprise Painted Buntings.
9–11 am
4WD South Beach drive Signature PINS experience
Deflate to ~20 psi and drive south from Malaquite — 4WD required past mile 5. Up to 60 miles of driveable beach, nesting Least Terns and Wilson's Plovers along the wrack line, virtually no other people past mile 10. Your partner drives, you shoot from the passenger window. Stop wherever birds present themselves. The RF 200-800 at 1280mm equivalent on open tidal flats earns every dollar of the rental.
⚠ Walk your spot before parking — avoid soft sand. Carry extra water — no facilities past mile 5. Re-inflate before leaving the beach.
Noon
Optional: move to PINS camp, or return to Mustang Island Choice point
If you want the PINS overnight experience: after the beach drive, check into Bird Island Basin ($8, vault toilets, Laguna Madre bay views, gravel sites — no reservations, first-come) or self-register for South Beach primitive camping (free with ATB pass, Gulf-facing, total wilderness). Both are 15 min from Malaquite. If you'd rather stay comfortable: return to Mustang Island — hookups, hot showers, reliable spot.
1–4 pm
Beach time · swim · Laguna Madre flats Afternoon freedom
Malaquite Beach for swimming — vehicle-free, clean Gulf water at ~78–82°F in late May. Or the Laguna Madre side at Bird Island Basin for calmer flat water. Late afternoon: scan the tidal flats on the bay side — low tide in the evening light is ideal for Reddish Egret hunting and Long-billed Curlew on exposed mudflats. Check tide chart for timing.
5:30 pm
Evening tidal flats birding + sunset at camp Golden hour
The hour before sunset is the best photography light of the day. Work the Laguna Madre edge — Reddish Egret doing its lunging dance in the shallows is one of the most entertaining and photogenic behaviors in North American birding. Then return to camp for a camp dinner and genuine wilderness sunset — Gulf side for the last light, or Laguna Madre side for the color over the bay.
Evening
Camp dinner at PINS (or Mustang Island) Keep it simple
Cook at camp — tonight is about the experience, not the restaurant. Grill on the tailgate, watch stars emerge (PINS has genuinely dark skies with minimal light pollution), listen to the Gulf or the Laguna Madre depending on which side you're on. Have a cold drink and review the day's shots. Early night — sunrise birding tomorrow.

Swap-in options

Optional Activities

These replace or supplement the existing schedule depending on your energy and interests. None are required — the core itinerary is complete without them.

Saturday afternoon · swap for beach time 🛶
Laguna Madre kayak / SUP
Rentals available in Port Aransas (~15 min from PINS). Flat, warm bay water. Eye-level with Reddish Egrets and Black Skimmers from the water — extraordinary photographic perspective. Best done 10am–1pm before heat peaks. ~$50 for 2 hrs.
Friday afternoon · replaces Paradise Pond 🔬
PINS Malaquite on Friday afternoon
After work at 3pm, drive straight to PINS (15 min) instead of staying in town. Pick up your tide chart at Malaquite Visitor Center (open until 5pm), walk the Grasslands Trail (0.75 mi), and scout Bird Island Basin for Saturday. Gives you a head start on the park before the big Saturday session.
Saturday morning · before birding 🌅
PINS South Jetty fishing pier at dawn
A short walk from the Malaquite parking area. Brown Pelicans and Magnificent Frigatebird visible from the pier at first light. No fishing license needed for the pier observation — just walk out and scan. Great vantage for seabirds before the beach drive.
Saturday evening · at camp 🌟
PINS stargazing
PINS South Beach has genuinely dark skies with minimal light pollution — on a clear night, the Milky Way is visible over the Gulf. After dinner, walk out to the tide line and let your eyes adjust. Common Nighthawk calling overhead, occasional nightjar on the dunes. One of the unexpected highlights of a beach camp night.
Friday evening · after dinner 🍺
Port Aransas town walk + waterfront
After Tortuga's, walk the Port Aransas waterfront — Roberts Point Park overlooks Turtle Cove and the marina. The Tarpon Inn (1900, historic fishing lodge) is worth a peek for its wall of tarpon scales signed by famous guests including FDR. A 15-minute walk from Tortuga's.
Saturday morning · add-on 🐢
Sea turtle nest monitoring patrol
Park volunteers patrol PINS beach at dawn looking for nesting females and fresh tracks. Walk Malaquite Beach early enough and you may encounter an active patrol — rangers are happy to explain the program. If the Hatchling Hotline (361-949-7163) shows an imminent release, rearrange your morning entirely around it.
